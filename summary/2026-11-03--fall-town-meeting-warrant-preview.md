## Summary
Town Administrator Bob Rittenauer previewed the eight-article warrant for the Special Town Meeting on Monday, November 16, 2026, at 6 p.m. at the Dennis-Yarmouth Intermediate School on Station Avenue in South Yarmouth. He described it as a "town meeting light" cleanup session. Big long-term financial items are normally saved for the April annual meeting, while the fall meeting handles time-sensitive spending and zoning. No votes were taken, since this was an informational overview ahead of the meeting.

The most consequential items are the zoning articles. Articles 5, 6 and 7 deal with short-term rentals, because the existing bylaw expires on January 1, 2027 under a sunset clause. Article 5 would adopt a new bylaw with stronger regulations and enforcement. Article 6 would extend the sunset by one year so there is no gap in regulation. Article 7 is a fallback that would extend the existing bylaw if Articles 5 and 6 do not pass. Article 8 would amend the accessory dwelling unit (ADU) bylaw to comply with a new, more liberal state law that takes precedence over local rules.

The first four articles are financial. Article 1 transfers money for a new ambulance, Article 2 covers golf division capital renovations, Article 3 creates a new revolving fund bylaw with two funds, and Article 4 is the annual deposit into the wastewater stabilization fund. The administrator said the ambulance and golf items are paid from user and ambulance receipts rather than taxes, and that the fall timing lets the work happen over the winter.

## Key Points & Votes
### Meeting logistics
- **Special Town Meeting: Monday, November 16, 2026, 6 p.m.**, Dennis-Yarmouth Intermediate School, Station Avenue, South Yarmouth.
- **Eight articles** in total; each is discussed and voted on separately.
- The warrant is available at yarmouth.ma.us via the red banner on the homepage.

### Articles 1–2: Ambulance and golf
- **Article 1:** transfer for a **new ambulance**, funded from ambulance receipts, not taxpayers. Approval in the fall allows construction to begin over the winter because of long lead times.
- **Article 2:** capital renovations for the golf division, paid from **golf user fees** with no impact on taxes. Work can be finished over the winter before the spring season.

### Article 3: Revolving funds bylaw
- **Article 3** would establish a new revolving fund bylaw with **two revolving funds**.
- One fund would hold fees from events at **Parker's River Landing Park** to pay for upkeep and cleanup.
- The other would hold fees paid by other Cape departments to use Yarmouth's **new firefighter training facility**, covering training and maintenance.
- Annual amounts are limited by law and purposes must be specified in detail.

### Article 4: Wastewater stabilization fund
- **Article 4** is the annual contribution to the wastewater stabilization fund, with a goal of setting aside about **$2 million** a year from reserves to offset future debt service.
- Phase one of the wastewater project is **over 60% complete**, and the town has met its pre-funding targets.

### Articles 5–7: Short-term rentals
- **Article 5:** new short-term rental bylaw replacing the old one, with stronger neighborhood protections and **tougher enforcement**. The use remains permitted.
- **Article 6:** **extends the existing sunset by one year** until the new bylaw fully takes effect.
- **Article 7:** backup measure to **extend the existing bylaw** if Articles 5 and 6 fail.
- The existing bylaw sunsets **January 1, 2027**. The Planning Board and Town Planner will present the articles.

### Article 8: Accessory dwelling units
- **Article 8** amends the ADU bylaw to align with a new state law, while keeping local regulations that remain allowed.
- An ADU is a standalone unit with its own kitchen and sleeping area on the same lot as a principal home.

## Takeaways
- Taxes: the ambulance, golf, and revolving fund items are described as funded by user fees and receipts, not taxes. The wastewater deposit comes from town reserves.
- Short-term rentals: without action, regulations would lapse on January 1, 2027. Residents can expect stricter rules and stronger enforcement if Article 5 passes, with Articles 6 and 7 as safeguards against a gap.
- Development and sewer: the ADU changes bring local zoning into line with state law, and phase one of the sewer project is over 60% complete with continued pre-funding to manage future debt costs.
