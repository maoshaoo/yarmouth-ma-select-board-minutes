## Summary
Town Administrator Bob Rittenauer previewed the eight-article warrant for the Special Town Meeting on Monday, November 16, 2026, at 6 p.m. at the Dennis-Yarmouth Intermediate School on Station Avenue in South Yarmouth. He described it as a "town meeting light" and a cleanup-style session. Major financial items are normally saved for the April annual meeting, and the fall meeting is used for time-sensitive spending and for zoning work. Because Yarmouth has an open town meeting, every registered voter who attends can vote on each article.

The most consequential items are the short-term rental articles (5, 6 and 7). The existing short-term rental bylaw has a sunset clause and expires January 1, 2027. Article 5 would adopt a new bylaw with stronger neighborhood protections and tougher enforcement provisions. The use would remain permitted. Article 6 would extend the sunset for one year so there is no gap in regulation while the new bylaw takes effect. Article 7 is a backup that would extend the existing bylaw if Articles 5 and 6 do not pass. The Planning Board and Town Planner Kathy Williams are expected to present these at length.

Article 8 would amend the town's accessory dwelling unit (ADU) bylaw to comply with a new state law that is somewhat more permissive. State law takes precedence over local bylaws, so the amendment is meant to align the two while keeping the town's other local regulations. The administrator said he would prefer that zoning be left to local governments.

The four financial articles come first. Article 1 would transfer money from ambulance receipts to buy a new ambulance. Article 2 covers golf division capital renovations paid from user fees. Article 3 creates a new revolving fund bylaw with two funds. Article 4 is the annual contribution to the wastewater stabilization fund. The administrator said the first three are not funded by taxpayers.

## Key Points & Votes
No votes were taken. This was a preview of articles that voters will act on November 16.

### Meeting logistics
- **Special Town Meeting: Monday, November 16, 2026, 6 p.m.**, Dennis-Yarmouth Intermediate School, Station Avenue, South Yarmouth.
- The warrant is available through the red banner on yarmouth.ma.us.
- **Eight articles**, each discussed and voted on separately.

### Article 1: Ambulance purchase
- Transfer for a new ambulance, funded from the **ambulance receipts fund**, not taxes.
- It is on the fall warrant because ambulances are built from scratch and have a long lead time.

### Article 2: Golf division capital
- Renovations funded by **golf user fees**, with no tax impact.
- Fall approval lets work be done over the winter, ahead of the spring golf season.

### Article 3: Revolving fund bylaw
- Creates a new bylaw with **two revolving funds**.
- One uses event fees from private organizers at Parker's River Landing Park for upkeep and cleanup.
- The other uses fees paid by other Cape Cod departments that train at the new firefighter training facility.
- Annual spending limits keep the funds small and manageable.

### Article 4: Wastewater stabilization fund
- Continues the pre-funding program, with a goal of setting aside about **$2 million a year** from reserves to offset future debt service.
- Phase 1 of the wastewater project is **over 60% complete**.
- The town has met all of its funding targets.

### Articles 5–7: Short-term rentals
- **Article 5:** new short-term rental bylaw replacing the one that sunsets January 1, 2027, with stronger regulation and enforcement.
- **Article 6:** extends the existing sunset by **one year** to ensure continuity.
- **Article 7:** a fallback that extends the existing bylaw if Articles 5 and 6 fail.

### Article 8: Accessory dwelling units
- Amends the local ADU bylaw to conform to the new state law.
- The town keeps its other local regulations.

## Takeaways
- **Taxes:** The ambulance, golf and revolving-fund articles are funded by user fees, ambulance receipts and event or training fees rather than taxes. The wastewater set-aside continues to be funded from town reserves.
- **Short-term rentals:** The current rules expire January 1, 2027. Residents who care about neighborhood impacts such as parking and overcrowding should review Articles 5–7 and attend, since the outcome determines what rules apply next year.
- **Wastewater and housing:** Wastewater Phase 1 is described as more than 60% complete, with debt costs being prepared for in advance. The ADU changes bring local zoning in line with state law, which may make ADUs easier to build.
