## Summary

The Select Board's July 21, 2026 meeting opened with a long run of recognitions. Retiring Police, Fire and Public Works employees received citations, as did the Yarmouth Historical Society, the Historical Commission and the Community Development team for the 250th anniversary events. Seafood Sam's received the Commission on Disability's Evelyn M. Beal Impact Award. The board also unanimously confirmed Tara Monroe, a 20-year assessing employee, as Director of Assessing. She succeeds the retiring director, and the board and Town Administrator described the move as a successful example of succession planning.

The board's main business was licensing and planning. It approved a liquor license transfer and a new entertainment license for the Irish Village at 476 Route 28, with outdoor music restricted after neighbors raised noise concerns. It also approved a special entertainment license for the police department's National Night Out and a new fortune teller license. The board then heard a two-year coastal resiliency grant wrap-up for the Route 28 low-lying corridor. It voted to support a next-round state and federal grant application and a letter to MassDOT asking that resiliency strategies be included in planned Route 28 work.

Other items included accepting a **$31,000** donation from the Last Call Foundation for the regional fire training building and approving the Chasebrook Park signage. The board began reworking the town's park and property naming policy but referred it back to staff, with a vote expected August 11. The meeting closed with board appointments and resignations, a consent agenda amendment to the Town Administrator's contract, and a preview of the town meeting calendar and the goals workshop held July 18.

## Key Points & Votes

### Recognitions and Proclamations
- Citations were read for retiring police Detective Eric Nuss and Sergeants Andrew Coville and George Sokolos. The Fire Department honored Firefighter/Paramedic Keith Collins for over 33 years of service.
- DPW Director Jeff Colby recognized retiring Parks and Cemetery Supervisor Kevin Bucklin (11 years) and heavy truck driver Dan Hansen (10 years). A board member said retaining and recruiting staff should be considered in upcoming budgeting.
- Seafood Sam's received the Evelyn M. Beal Impact Award from the Commission on Disability for accessibility features.
- The Historical Society, Historical Commission and Community Development team were honored for the June 20 Revolutionary War monument dedication at Thatcher Park, the grave marker dedication and wreath-laying at Ancient Cemetery, and related events.

### Director of Assessing
- **Board unanimously affirmed the Town Administrator's appointment of Tara Monroe as Director of Assessing.** The Town Administrator noted that under the charter the board may decline an appointment only by a four-fifths vote.

### Licenses and Hearings
- **National Night Out:** **Unanimously approved a special entertainment license, with the fee waived,** for the police department event at Parkers River Landing on August 4, 5–8 p.m.
- **Fortune teller:** **Unanimously approved a new license** for a sole proprietor on Route 28 in West Yarmouth. Staff confirmed the statutory residency requirement, business certificate and building department review were completed.
- **Irish Village (476 Route 28):** **Approved the transfer of the all-alcohol on-premises hotel liquor license** from SNH Hotel Yarmouth LLC (the Cape Point) to Cape Aden Inc., with Thomas McCormick as manager.
  - **Indoor entertainment: noon to 12:30 a.m.**
  - **Outdoor entertainment: one-piece acoustic music only, no amplification, no drums, 1–9 p.m. Monday–Saturday and 1–8 p.m. Sundays.**
  - The applicant corrected the application, which had requested outdoor music until 11 p.m. Two abutters objected to late outdoor amplified music and one letter supported the application. A neighbor who spoke accepted the revised limits.
  - Board members and the chair told neighbors to contact the owner first, then the police, and that violations can bring the license back before the board.

### Coastal Resiliency Grant (Route 28 Corridor)
- Town Engineer Amanda Lima and a Woods Hole Group consultant reviewed the two-year MVP grant, about **$178,000** in total. Year one modeled emergency access and found Route 28 causes the most disruption in flood scenarios. Year two inventoried about 2,073 structures and found about 32 would likely flood in a typical year by 2030, rising to about 696 in a Sandy-type storm.
- The work produced a list of 11 priority roads. Board members, citing public safety access, pressed for Great Island causeway access to remain part of the discussion. The Fire Chief said the Great Island Association is working on its own studies.
- **Approved supporting DPW's pursuit of state and federal funds, including an MVP application in the next round (winter 2027), and a prioritization plan for focused roadway projects, and authorizing the chair to sign a letter.**
- **Approved supporting work with MassDOT to include resiliency strategies in proposed Route 28 work between the Barnstable town line and the Parkers River Bridge, and authorizing the chair to sign a letter.**

### Fire Training Building Donation
- **Accepted a $31,000 donation from the Last Call Foundation** to help finish the regional fire training building, a project in the works since 2018. The Fire Chief said the money gives a cushion against cost increases, particularly for gas-fired training props.

### Chasebrook Park Signage
- During public comment, a resident urged the board to keep donor recognition on the park signs.
- **Approved the new Chasebrook Park signage, consistent with the Parkers River Landing and Sandy Pond signs, and directed staff to find a way to honor the original donors and park history elsewhere in the park.** Ideas included a boulder with a plaque, interpretive signs and the park website. QR codes were set aside because of town IT concerns.

### Naming Policy
- The board discussed an updated draft policy to replace the 2008 policy. It debated the clause exempting properties controlled by other boards, a possible signature requirement for public suggestions, and how public comment and hearings should work.
- **Referred the draft to staff and legal counsel to revise and return, with the vote on the August 11 agenda.**

### Appointments and Resignations
- **Accepted resignations** of Eric Weston from the Board of Health and Mike Duffy from the Cape Light Compact governing board alternate position.
- **Appointed** Christine Dolan to the Conservation Commission (term to June 2029) and Michael Ahern as Cape Light Compact alternate (through December 31, 2026).
- **Reappointed** two Historical Commission members for three years, one Historical Commission member for one year, and two Recreation Commission members (one for three years, one for one year).

### Administration and Consent Agenda
- **Approved the consent agenda as amended:** the Town Administrator's employment agreement extension now runs through December 31, 2027, with the compensation provisions in the draft left in place. Board members were initially unsure how the draft dates matched their earlier executive session vote.
- The board plans to formally address the status of the former library building committee at a future meeting. A draft of the goals from the July 18 workshop is expected at the next meeting. August 11 will also bring a vote to set the November 16 town meeting date. Two board members will attend an August 10 meeting with the school committee.

## Takeaways
- **Flooding and wastewater-adjacent infrastructure planning is moving toward funding requests.** The Route 28 corridor and a short list of low-lying roads are being positioned for state and federal grants. Residents and businesses can expect property-level flood information to be posted online.
- **Entertainment licensing balances business and neighbors.** The Irish Village can host indoor music late, but outdoor music is limited to acoustic and ends by 9 p.m. (8 p.m. Sundays). The board signaled it will act on violations.
- **Staffing transitions and parks signage and naming are in focus.** Several long-serving employees have retired, and the board linked this to retention and budgeting. The naming policy, the Chasebrook Park sign and the town meeting date all return soon.
