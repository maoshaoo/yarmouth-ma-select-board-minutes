## Summary

The Select Board's July 21, 2026 meeting combined a long run of recognitions with several licensing and policy decisions. The board approved a liquor license transfer and entertainment license for the Irish Village at 476 Route 28 in West Yarmouth. Outdoor music was limited to one acoustic, unamplified performer, with hours matching the earlier 2022 conditions. After neighbors raised noise concerns, the applicant corrected his request, which had listed outdoor entertainment until 11 p.m. The board also approved two motions on coastal flood resilience: one supporting a next-round state grant application, and one supporting a letter asking MassDOT to build resiliency into its Route 28 work.

The board confirmed Tara Monroe as Director of Assessing, succeeding the retiring Andy Machado, and accepted a $31,000 donation from the Last Call Foundation toward the regional fire training building. It approved a revised approach to the Chasebrook Park signs, and agreed the draft naming policy needs more work before a vote. Smaller approvals included a National Night Out entertainment license with the fee waived, a new fortune teller license, board and commission appointments, and an amended extension of the Town Administrator's employment agreement.

The first part of the meeting recognized retiring town employees: three police officers, a firefighter-paramedic, and two Public Works employees. The board also honored Seafood Sam's with the Evelyn M. Beal Impact Award for accessibility, and the Historical Society, Historical Commission, and Community Development team for the town's 250th anniversary events.

## Key Points & Votes

### Irish Village liquor and entertainment license (476 Route 28)
- **Approved the transfer of the all-alcohol on-premises hotel liquor license** from SNH Hotel Yarmouth LLC (the Cape Point) to Cape Aden Inc., doing business as the Irish Village. **Thomas McCormick was approved as manager.**
- **Entertainment license approved with conditions:**
  - Indoors: noon to **12:30 a.m.**
  - Outdoors: **one-piece acoustic music only, no amplification and no drums, 1–9 p.m. Monday–Saturday and 1–8 p.m. Sunday.**
- The applicant said the 11 p.m. outdoor request in the notice was a mistake and that most music is indoors.
- Two abutters wrote in opposition, citing past loud outdoor music and late-night pool noise, and one letter supported the application. Two neighbors spoke, and both said they were comfortable with the restrictions. Board members said noise complaints should go to the manager first, then to police, and that violations can bring the license back before the board.

### Coastal resilience (MVP grant) and Route 28
- The town engineer and a Woods Hole Group consultant reviewed the two-year state Municipal Vulnerability Preparedness grant (about **$178,000**). The work covered emergency-access analysis for flooded roads, structure-level flood vulnerability for roughly 2,000 properties, and a public resiliency fair. They identified 11 priority roads, with Route 28 causing the most service disruption.
- **Approved a motion supporting DPW's pursuit of state and federal funds, including an MVP application in the winter 2027 round, and authorizing the chair to sign a letter.**
- **Approved a motion supporting work with MassDOT to include resiliency strategies along Route 28 between the Barnstable town line and the Parkers River Bridge, and authorizing the chair to sign a letter.**
- Board members pressed to keep Great Island causeway access on the radar for public safety. The fire chief said the department has a water-level sensor there and is coordinating with the Great Island Association.

### Fire training building donation
- **Accepted a $31,000 donation from the Last Call Foundation** to help finish the regional fire training building, a project underway since 2018. The money is expected to cover gas-fired training props and give some cost buffer.

### Chasebrook Park signs
- A resident urged the board to keep recognition of the original donors who helped save the park.
- **Approved the sign design as presented, consistent with Parker's River Landing and Sandy Pond signage, and directed staff to find a way to honor the original donors elsewhere in the park.** Ideas included a boulder with a plaque or interpretive signs. Staff said IT discourages QR codes, so a web page is an alternative.

### Draft naming policy
- The board reviewed a draft replacing the 2008 policy. Members generally agreed to strike the sentence exempting properties controlled by other boards, to make clear the Select Board names town property, and to keep a public comment period.
- Open questions included the proposed petition signatures (the draft says 50, with discussion of certification) and whether a "public hearing" should be advertised.
- **Referred to staff for a revised draft; a vote is planned for August 11.**

### Personnel, licenses, and appointments
- **Confirmed Tara Monroe as Director of Assessing** (unanimous). She has worked in the assessing division since 2007 and has been assistant assessor since 2019.
- **Approved a special entertainment license for the police department's National Night Out**, Aug. 4, 5–8 p.m. at Parker's River Landing, with the fee waived (unanimous).
- **Approved a new fortune teller license** for Donna Eli, doing business as Miss Donna's Mystical Astrology, at 615 Route 28 (unanimous).
- **Accepted resignations** from the Board of Health (Eric Weston) and the Cape Light Compact alternate representative position (Mike Duffy).
- **Appointments:** Christine Dolan to Conservation Commission (to June 2029); Michael Ahern as Cape Light Compact alternate (through Dec. 31, 2026). **Reappointments:** Historical Commission members Julie Makabe and David Genocopoulos (to July 2029) and Sam Lawrence (to July 2027); Recreation Commission members Mary Johnson (to July 2029) and Debbie Clark (to July 2027). Name spellings come from a machine transcript.
- **Amended and approved the consent agenda,** changing the Town Administrator's employment agreement so it extends through **December 31, 2027** instead of the draft's one-year extension. The merit and cost-of-living increases effective April 5, 2027 stayed in place.

- The board plans to take up a draft of this year's goals (from the July 18 goals workshop) at its next meeting. It will set a Town Meeting date on August 11, with a proposed Town Meeting on November 16, 2026.
- Two board members will represent the board at the August 10 Dennis-Yarmouth school district meeting.
- The board discussed formally confirming the disbanding of the library building committee, with a notice possibly on a future appointments agenda.

## Takeaways

- **Neighborhood noise limits are tied to the Irish Village license.** Outdoor music at the former Cape Point site is restricted to quiet, unamplified solo acoustic sets ending by 9 p.m. (8 p.m. Sundays), and violations can bring the license back before the board.
- **The town is positioning for flood-resilience funding along Route 28 and low-lying roads.** Staff will pursue state grants and press MassDOT on resiliency, with the 11 priority roads and public-safety access (including Great Island) driving priorities. Board members noted that state money may come with conditions.
- **The town is also managing staff turnover and outside funding.** Several long-serving employees retired and the board stressed the cost of recruiting and training replacements. An outside foundation is helping close a gap on the fire training building, and the Town Administrator's contract now runs through the end of 2027.
