## Summary

The Select Board spent most of the evening on wastewater. Public Works staff and the consultant team said the state Department of Environmental Protection (DEP) has permitted only 370,000 gallons per day for discharge at 99 Buck Island Road. Connecting everyone in Phase One needs about 420,000. Staff presented three options for adding discharge at the Baybury Hills golf course, costing roughly $17 million, $25 million, and $45 million, on top of the roughly $207 million already committed. Board members, led by Mark, said they would rather press DEP hard (regionally, and up to the secretary and governor's office) than commit to a new discharge site in the Aquifer Protection District. The town administrator will escalate with DEP and neighboring towns. No vote was taken on the Baybury Hills options.

The board also gave its final review to the special town meeting warrant. The Planning Board presented four articles: a new short-term rental general bylaw (Article 5), a zoning article continuing short-term rentals (Article 6), a backup article extending the sunset clause (Article 7), and an accessory dwelling unit (ADU) update to match state law (Article 8). Short-term rental rules in zoning expire November 30 if not modified, which would also put the rental excise tax at risk. The board recommended all eight articles and approved and executed the warrant. Mark abstained on Article 5, saying he opposes allowing commercialization of residential areas and wants a broader housing strategy, including incentives for year-round leasing.

The board unanimously approved a new annual all-alcohol restaurant license for Kashari Indian Restaurant and Bar at 39 Todd Road, with a condition that required documentation be provided at annual renewal. A neighboring golf property raised parking concerns and said it would appeal. Staff and the board noted that the building commissioner found the restaurant use grandfathered by earlier zoning relief. They also said the board has no authority over parking in a license decision, so that dispute goes through the zoning appeal process.

The board also voted to relax the 14-day temporary sign limit for Route 28 businesses in the sewer construction zone. It then voted to support staff developing a plan for limited temporary signs on town property. The administrator also previewed a tough school budget (a possible 6% increase against 2% to 2.5% revenue growth) and a school-bus camera ticketing pilot.

## Key Points & Votes

### Wastewater / DEP discharge permit
- DEP permitted **370,000 gallons per day** at Buck Island Road. Phase One needs **420,000**. Modeling suggests the site can handle over **1 million**, but DEP has not agreed.
- Baybury Hills options: **about $17 million** (enough for Phase One), **about $25 million** (full 1.8 million gallons per day), **about $45 million** (adds advanced treatment so the water can be used for irrigation).
- Funding timeline: staff said the State Revolving Fund (SRF) intended use plan comes out early 2027, and keeping the project on it would need **town meeting funding this spring**. Zero-percent SRF financing was described as worth about **$15.5 million** over the life of the $25 million option.
- Staff warned that if Phase One customers can't all connect, betterment revenue falls and debt service could be at risk.
- **No vote taken.** Board members asked the town administrator to escalate to DEP leadership and neighboring towns. The Water Resources Advisory Committee takes up the question Monday.

### Wastewater construction update
- Overall Phase One is about **60% complete**. Treatment plant substantial completion is **March 2028**, slipped by a couple of months.
- Contracts 2 to 4 are essentially done. Contract 5 is under way near Captain Parker's. Contract 6 road excavation and a Route 28 closure begin **Monday, November 2**.
- Staff are re-examining the eight-phase plan because DEP's standards changed. Results are expected early in the new year.
- A board member urged continued attention to business interruption funding.

### Special town meeting articles (short-term rentals and ADUs)
- The Planning Board voted unanimously to send Articles 5 to 8 to town meeting.
- Article 5, the general bylaw, takes a majority vote and carries no grandfathering. It would take effect **January 1, 2028** and adds:
  - annual building inspections;
  - parking and occupancy rules and listing requirements;
  - the ability to revoke a rental certificate for up to two years after three separate verified violations, appealable to the Select Board.
- Limits on how many rentals one owner may hold were dropped for further study.
- Article 8 brings zoning into line with state ADU law. It keeps a **350 square foot** minimum and the primary-residence requirement, though town counsel cautioned these could be flagged by the Attorney General. Six ADUs have been completed and 15 applications are active.
- **Recommended Articles 1 to 4** (fire department capital, golf capital, revolving fund bylaw, wastewater stabilization fund), **unanimously**.
- **Recommended Articles 5 to 8**, with one abstention on Article 5.
- **Approved and executed the warrant**, unanimously.

### Liquor license hearing
- **Approved unanimously** a new annual all-alcohol on-premise license for Kashari Indian Restaurant and Bar, with a condition for documentation at annual renewal.
- Restaurant capacity under the common victualler license was reduced to **49 seats**.

### Temporary signs on Route 28
- **Approved** a waiver of the 14-day temporary sign limit, through about May 31, for businesses in the construction zone (permits still required).
- **Approved** a motion supporting staff developing a plan for temporary signs on town property, with a per-business limit.

### Other actions
- Accepted a resignation from a town committee; approved minutes for December 16, 2025 and January 6 and 13, 2026 (with a footer correction); approved the consent agenda.
- Upcoming agenda items: liquor license regulation updates, fee review (including beach fees), and a possible Parker's River Landing event presentation at the November 10 workshop.

### Administrator's report
- Early school budget estimates show up to a **6% increase**, versus total revenue growth of **2% to 2.5%**; departments will be asked to trim.
- A school bus safety-arm camera ticketing pilot is being developed, and an education stabilization fund for the fines was floated.
- An energy and climate committee plans a spring town meeting article for a carbon-free roadmap.

## Takeaways

- **Sewer costs may grow:** Phase One may need an extra **$17 million to $25 million or more** beyond the $207 million. Because DEP discharge limits could block some connections, voters may face a funding decision at spring town meeting and possibly a property tax component.
- **Short-term rentals:** Yarmouth's rental rules lapse November 30 unless town meeting acts. The proposed rules add inspections, occupancy and parking limits, and suspension powers, while an ownership cap is deferred. Part of the excise tax funds wastewater, and some board members want more housing incentives and enforcement staffing.
- **Route 28 disruption:** New closures begin November 2, and the town is easing sign rules and expanding outreach to help affected businesses. Next year's budget will be tight, with the schools' requests outpacing revenue growth.
