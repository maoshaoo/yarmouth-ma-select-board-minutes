## Summary

The Select Board spent most of the evening on two major items. First, it reviewed the four Planning Board articles (short-term rentals and accessory dwelling units) for the special town meeting. Later, it recommended all eight warrant articles and signed the warrant. Second, it heard a wastewater update that included a new, costly decision on where to discharge treated effluent. The board did not decide the discharge question, but it signaled it wants to push the state Department of Environmental Protection (DEP) hard before committing money.

On short-term rentals (STRs), the Planning Board explained that the current zoning rules that allow STRs expire November 30 unless town meeting acts, which would cost the town STR excise tax revenue. About 75% of that revenue goes to the wastewater program. The package has three layers. A new general bylaw, passable by majority vote, would take effect January 1, 2028. A zoning article would continue the STR use and extend the sunset to December 31, 2027. A backup article would extend the sunset if the first two fail. The general bylaw adds an annual building inspection, a parking limit based on what the site can hold, 11 p.m.–7 a.m. quiet hours and overnight occupancy limits, listing requirements, and stronger enforcement. Enforcement includes revocation of a rental certificate for up to two years after three verified material violations, appealable to the Select Board. A proposed one-STR-per-owner limit was dropped for now. A separate article updates the zoning bylaw to match the state's by-right accessory dwelling unit (ADU) mandate. One board member voiced strong concern about commercialization of residential neighborhoods and urged a broader approach to year-round housing. The board later recommended all four articles, with one abstention on the general bylaw.

The wastewater discussion centered on discharge capacity. DEP has permitted 370,000 gallons per day at the Buck Island Road site, but connecting everyone in Phase One requires about 420,000. Staff presented options for adding discharge at the Baybury Hills golf course, at an estimated $17 million, $25 million or $45 million, on top of the roughly $207 million already invested. Board members objected to discharging in an aquifer protection district up-gradient of drinking water wells. They also questioned DEP's reliance on a guidance document rather than regulation, and the board's direction was to escalate the issue, working with other Cape towns. Staff cautioned that a failure to connect all of Phase One would hurt betterment revenue and debt payments, and that funding approval at spring town meeting would be needed to stay within the state revolving fund (SRF) cycle.

The board also approved a new all-alcohol restaurant license for a South Yarmouth restaurant over a neighbor's parking objections, and it approved temporary-sign relief for Route 28 businesses affected by sewer construction.

## Key Points & Votes

### Special Town Meeting Articles (STRs and ADUs)
- Planning Board members said the articles were approved unanimously by the board after multiple public hearings, including one September 23.
- The general bylaw (Article 5) would take effect **January 1, 2028**. Zoning Article 6 would extend the sunset to **December 31, 2027**. Article 7 is a backup sunset extension and would be withdrawn if not needed.
- STR registration stays annual through the health department, with a new annual building inspection and certificate of inspection.
- Overnight street parking is prohibited 11 p.m.–7 a.m., and parking on lawns and front green spaces is barred.
- Listings must include the town rental certificate, the state Department of Revenue number, parking and occupancy limits, and a no-large-events notice.
- Article 8 (ADUs) codifies the state mandate. It keeps a **350 sq. ft. minimum** unit size and a primary-residence requirement, though town counsel warned both may be flagged by the Attorney General. ADUs and the principal dwelling on an ADU lot cannot be STRs.
- Staff reported **6 ADUs completed and 15 applications active**.
- Board members raised the need for enforcement staffing and floated using STR revenue for it.
- **Recommended Articles 1 (fire capital), 2 (golf capital) and 3 (revolving fund bylaw) unanimously.** Article 4 (wastewater stabilization fund) was also recommended; no tally was stated.
- **Recommended Article 5 (STR general bylaw) with one abstention**, from the board member who opposes the commercialization of residential areas.
- **Recommended Articles 6, 7 and 8 unanimously, and approved and executed the town meeting warrant unanimously.**

### All-Alcohol License Hearing (Kashari Indian Restaurant and Bar, 39 Todd Road)
- A neighboring property owner (Davenport Realty Trust) argued the restaurant's parking is inadequate and the restaurant use had been abandoned. The owner said he would appeal if the license were granted.
- The building commissioner found the restaurant is a pre-existing non-conforming use under past ZBA decisions. The health department set capacity at **49 seats**.
- Board members said the board does not regulate parking through liquor licenses, and any zoning appeal would proceed separately.
- **Approved the new all-alcohol license unanimously, with the condition that the licensee provide annual proof of her green card extension at renewal.**

### Wastewater Update and Baybury Hills Options
- Buck Island Road permit: **370,000 gallons/day**. Phase One needs about **420,000**.
- Baybury Hills options: **$17 million** (enough for Phase One), **$25 million** (full 1.8 million gpd), **$45 million** (adds advanced treatment for irrigation reuse).
- Consultants said modeling shows a small fraction of effluent (under 0.1% to 4%) could reach drinking water wells over 21–240 years, with monitoring wells planned.
- No vote was taken. The board asked the Town Administrator to escalate with DEP, coordinate with Barnstable, Dennis and other Cape towns, and consider legal options later. The Water Resources Advisory Committee will take up the issue Monday.
- Construction is about **60% complete** overall. The treatment plant's substantial completion is **March 2028**. Contracts 2–4 are done, Contract 5 is due February 2028, and Contract 6 begins full road excavation **November 2** with a completion date of December 2028.
- Staff are reviewing the Phase 2 scope and nitrogen-removal phasing, with results expected early in the new year.

### Temporary Signs for Route 28 Businesses
- **Approved a waiver of the 14-day temporary sign limit** for businesses in the construction zone, through about May 31 (businesses still need permits).
- **Approved supporting staff in developing a plan for temporary signs on town property, with a limit per business.**

### Other Business
- Accepted a committee resignation with thanks and approved three sets of past minutes (with a footer correction). Approved the consent agenda.
- The Town Administrator reported that early school budget numbers show up to a **6% increase** against revenue growth of about 2–2.5%. He also reported a pilot program for automated school-bus stop-arm ticketing.
- A carbon-free roadmap proposal is expected for spring town meeting.

## Takeaways

- **STRs:** Town meeting action is needed before November 30 to keep STRs lawful in residential zones and preserve the excise tax that funds wastewater. Stricter inspection, parking, occupancy and enforcement rules would start in 2028. Ownership limits and commercialization concerns remain for future consideration.
- **Wastewater and taxes:** Phase One may not be able to connect every property under the current discharge permit. A further **$17–45 million** investment is under discussion on top of about $207 million spent. Voters may see a funding request at spring town meeting, and a property-tax component is possible.
- **Services and local business:** Route 28 sewer work will cause new road closures starting November 2, and the town is easing sign rules and expanding outreach to help businesses. Tight budgets are also expected, with school requests outpacing revenue growth.
