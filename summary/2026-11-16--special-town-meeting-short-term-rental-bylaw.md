## Summary

At the November 16, 2026 Special Town Meeting, Yarmouth voters took up eight articles. The most consequential was a new general bylaw (Chapter 110) regulating short-term rentals, which passed after the longest debate of the night. It moves the rules out of the zoning bylaw into a general bylaw, which needs only a majority vote to amend and allows stronger enforcement. Most of the new requirements take effect January 1, 2028. Voters then passed a companion zoning article that keeps short-term rentals allowed in zoning until the new bylaw takes effect, avoiding a gap when the existing provisions would otherwise have expired on November 30, 2026. Article 7, a backup extension of that expiration to the end of 2029, was withdrawn as no longer needed.

Debate on the short-term rental bylaw centered on its ban on short-term renting a property that has an accessory dwelling unit (ADU). Critics said this goes too far, infringes on property rights, and could push owners to rent out their main house and live off-Cape. Supporters, including several residents who spoke from neighborhood experience, said the bylaw is a step toward better enforcement and more year-round housing. One Select Board member abstained, saying the article did not go far enough to limit short-term rentals. Another board member replied that rejecting it would not leave the town better positioned to do more. Town staff noted the 2023 ADU rules mean the town wants ADUs used for year-round residents. Staff said the bylaw can be revisited at future town meetings.

Voters also approved Article 8, which updates the town's accessory dwelling unit zoning to comply with state law under the 2024 Affordable Homes Act. Several local requirements, such as special permits, minimum lot size, and owner occupancy, can no longer be enforced. The town kept the provisions it could, including setbacks, one ADU per lot, a 350-square-foot minimum size, no separate ownership, no short-term rental use, and limits on home businesses. The Attorney General will review the article and may strike any provisions that conflict with state law.

On financial items, voters approved spending from ambulance receipts for an ambulance replacement (Article 1) and $650,000 in golf capital projects from golf enterprise retained earnings (Article 2), neither requiring property tax dollars. They also approved spending limits for two revolving funds (Article 3) and an action on the Wastewater Stabilization Fund (Article 4). A resident urged a hold on Article 4 over uncertainty about where treated wastewater will be discharged. The article passed by a clear majority.

## Key Points & Votes

### Article 1 – Fire Department Capital (Ambulance)
- **Approved unanimously**, with the Select Board unanimous and the Finance Committee 5-0.
- Funds a replacement ambulance from ambulance receipts, with no property tax funds required. The Ambulance Reserve held **$2,627,034.58** as of September 30, 2026.

### Article 2 – Golf Capital Expenses
- **Approved unanimously** (Select Board 5-0, Finance Committee 7-0).
- Total of **$650,000** in maintenance and safety improvements, paid from Golf Enterprise retained earnings (balance **$3,791,560** as of October 14, 2026), so no property tax dollars are needed.

### Article 3 – Revolving Fund Bylaw
- **Passed by majority** with one abstention (Select Board 5-0, Finance Committee 7-0).
- Sets FY2027 spending limits of **$50,000** for the Fire Training Fund and **$100,000** for the Parker's River Landing Fund.
- A resident questioned whether revenue-generating departments gain an advantage over those that must request funds. Officials answered that the revolving funds keep fees from the facility's use dedicated to that facility's costs, and that the training facility will sell time to other towns.

### Article 4 – Wastewater Stabilization Fund
- **Passed by a clear majority on a voice and hand vote** (Select Board 5-0, Finance Committee 7-0). The transcript does not state the amount involved.
- The fund helps pay for the town's first wastewater treatment system and offset future debt service. Its balance was **$11,940,212** as of October 19, 2026.
- One resident asked to pause the program until the town settles where treated water will go, and objected to the possibility of using Bayberry Hills Golf Course.

### Article 5 – General Bylaw Chapter 110, Short-Term Rentals
- **Approved** (Select Board 4-0 with one abstention, Planning Board 6-0). No vote tally was given.
- Short-term rental excise revenue was about **$2.375 million** in FY26, roughly **4.4%** of the FY27 operating budget, with **75%** going to the wastewater program.
- About 800 short-term rentals were registered in 2026, under 5% of the town's roughly 17,550 housing units.
- **New ineligible units:** properties with an ADU, and timeshares or fractional ownership units. Existing exclusions stay (affordable-restricted units, code violations, and others).
- **Listings must show:** the town and state rental certificates, the maximum number of cars, no overnight street parking from 11 p.m. to 7 a.m., the maximum overnight occupancy set by the health department, the two-night minimum, and that large events are prohibited.
- **Other rule changes:** parking limited to what the property can physically hold, annual building inspection added, and a broader list of prohibited large events (graduation, bachelor/bachelorette, and fraternity/sorority parties).
- **Enforcement:** building, health, and police departments can enforce. Civil penalties go up to **$5,000**. A rental certificate can be suspended, revoked, or not renewed (suspension of up to two years) after three verified material violations in a calendar year, with an appeal to the Select Board.
- **Administration:** an online complaint form, more owner education, and expanded application information are planned.
- **Effective date:** January 1, 2028.

### Article 6 – Zoning Article, Short-Term Rentals
- **Passed by the required two-thirds vote** (Select Board 5-0, Planning Board 6-0).
- Continues short-term rental use in zoning in line with the new Chapter 110 and extends the existing zoning provisions' expiration until the new bylaw takes effect January 1, 2028.

### Article 7 – Sunset Extension
- **Withdrawn** by voice vote, since Article 6 passed.

### Article 8 – Accessory Dwelling Units (Zoning)
- **Passed by majority** (Select Board 5-0, Planning Board 6-0).
- Brings local zoning into line with state ADU regulations. The Attorney General will review it and may strike non-compliant provisions.
- A home office allowed with an ADU is limited to the town's home occupation rules: no customers, no signage, and only low-impact activity.

## Takeaways

- **Short-term rental rules will tighten, but not until 2028.** Owners get a year to prepare. After that they face listing requirements, annual building inspections, tougher penalties, and possible loss of their rental certificate for repeated violations.
- **Short-term rentals remain an important funding source.** They produce roughly $2.4 million a year in excise revenue, mostly dedicated to wastewater debt, so the town framed the bylaw as regulating the activity rather than curbing it sharply. Some members and residents argued for stronger limits, and the town said it expects to adjust the bylaw at future town meetings.
- **Wastewater remains a live concern.** Voters again backed the wastewater stabilization fund, which held about $11.9 million, while some residents raised unresolved questions about where treated water will be discharged. Separately, the ADU changes show state law now limits how much Yarmouth can restrict such units.
