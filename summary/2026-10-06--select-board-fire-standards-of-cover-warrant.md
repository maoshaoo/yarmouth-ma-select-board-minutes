## Summary

The Select Board met on October 6, 2026. After an executive session on collective bargaining with the police supervisors union, it heard two long presentations on public safety. The first was a regional 911 center update. The second, and the biggest item, was a consultant's "standards of cover" study of the Yarmouth Fire Department. The study recommends growing shift staffing from 13 to 18, and eventually 21, over about ten years. It also recommends planning a new Station 4 and fixing life-safety problems at Stations 1 and 2. Board members called the report valuable but sobering, and said the town's finances will force priorities. They did not vote on it. They asked the town administrator to look into the cost of a second means of egress (such as an outdoor stairway) for the older stations, and they await a separate feasibility study from the fire chief.

The board also did a second review of the draft warrant for the upcoming special town meeting, now at eight articles. It covers an ambulance replacement, golf capital projects, two new revolving funds, a $2 million wastewater stabilization transfer, and four planning articles on short-term rentals and accessory dwelling units (ADUs). Board members stressed that commercialization of residential neighborhoods is a serious concern. They said they want more public discussion on October 27, when the planning board returns. The board also unanimously adopted the Old King's Highway election warrant.

During public comment, the Dennis-Yarmouth superintendent presented a 10-year comparison of municipal and school budget growth and asked the board to develop a long-term school funding strategy or stand with the schools if an override is needed. The town administrator said later that the FY28 budget process has begun with essentially no room for growth. He also reported that the state DEP approved the hydrogeological study showing the Bayberry Hills golf course can take all the treated wastewater discharge the planned plant needs.

## Key Points & Votes

### Executive session
- **Roll call vote to enter executive session on strategy for collective bargaining with the police supervisors union: approved, all board members voting aye.**

### Public comment
- Human Resources and the Town Clerk will hold an accessible voting Q&A at Town Hall on Friday, October 16, 10–11 a.m.
- A Precinct 3 resident flagged a pending streetlight and sidewalk application for Winslow Gray Road, saying the area near the conservation land is very dark. He also noted the special town meeting is about 42 days away and is the town's third meeting this year.
- Superintendent Mark Smith's 10-year review of town reports found:
  - Dennis-Yarmouth (DY) school assessments grew an average of **3.96% per year**, with **five overrides** in ten years.
  - The municipal budget (Article 2) grew an average of **4.64% per year**.
  - Over the period the municipal budget rose **$18.67 million** versus **$14.4 million** for the DY assessment.
  - He said the district has cut **46 positions** in three years, and asked the board to develop a long-term funding strategy or stand behind an override if it chooses one.
- A resident criticized a state senator's duplex bill as unlikely to create affordable housing. Another commenter (online) praised the Wall That Heals event and endorsed the regional communication center.

### Regional emergency communication center
- The executive director of the Cape Cod Regional Emergency Communication Center (Barnstable, Yarmouth, Sandwich) said the working groups have launched. The facility is planned for Finney's Lane in Barnstable, with 11 console positions expandable to 15, and a target opening of about 2028–2029 (realistically 2030).
- Construction, estimated at **$15–20 million**, is expected to be covered by a state 911 development grant funded by the **$1.50** per-line surcharge, not local taxes. The Commonwealth would fund each town's assessment at 100% for three years, then **50%** (year 4) and **75%** (year 5), with the full share starting in year six.
- The assessment will be split 50% on call volume and 50% on population. A figure around **$100,000** was discussed as a rough illustration, with a real number expected in two to three months.
- Dispatchers will remain Town of Barnstable employees but member towns get a say in operations. Board members asked about staffing, training on local streets, language access, and room to grow. Several said the center could improve response times and save the town money.

### Fire department standards of cover study (CPSM)
- Findings:
  - Yarmouth has an ISO fire rating of **3**, and a Class 2 rating is considered achievable.
  - EMS drives most calls.
  - Fire calls rose over 10% and EMS calls about 5% over three years.
  - About 5–6 facilities generate roughly **25% of calls**.
  - **57%** of calls overlap with another call, and about **6.5%** overlap with three or more other calls, leaving no available resources.
  - Dispatch, turnout and travel times exceed national benchmarks (for example, 90th-percentile first-engine travel time of 474 seconds against a 240-second benchmark).
- Recommendations:
  - Grow shift staffing **13 → 18 → 21** over about ten years, starting with a fourth person on the tower in 2027.
  - Add a dedicated, non-cross-staffed ambulance.
  - Plan a new **Station 4** (about 2033) as a possible headquarters.
  - Eventually replace Stations 1 and 2 (built 1960 and 1940). Station 1 is in a hurricane surge zone.
  - Add a second means of egress at the older stations (more serious at Station 2) and sprinklers if they are kept.
  - Adopt response targets of 240 seconds near stations and 360 seconds farther out, and a 600-second full first-alarm assembly.
  - Step up fire inspections, which would be needed if short-term rental inspections are added (roughly 2,000 more per year).
- Board discussion:
  - Members were concerned about cost and some questioned whether four stations is realistic. Others said it is important to know the options.
  - They discussed fees for repeat EMS responses to facilities, and noted state limits and private ambulance risks.
  - They agreed to await the chief's feasibility study and a priorities list.
  - **The board asked the town administrator to research the cost of a second egress (outdoor stairway or a rental in the meantime) for the fire stations.**

### Old King's Highway election
- **Warrant for the November 30, 2026 Old King's Highway annual meeting and election: approved unanimously.**
- **Nomination paper deadlines: approved unanimously.** Papers must be obtained by Friday, Nov. 20 at 4:30 p.m. and returned by Tuesday, Nov. 24 at 4:30 p.m.

### Special town meeting warrant (second draft, eight articles)
- Article 1 funds an ambulance replacement from ambulance receipts, with no property tax (the ordering lead time is about 800 days). Another capital item is for golf (Bass River and Bayberry bathrooms and cart paths). Both are in the fall so work can be done in the off-season.
- Article 3 creates two revolving funds, for the fire training facility and Parkers River Landing park.
- Article 4 would move **$2 million** from not-yet-certified free cash to the wastewater stabilization fund.
- Short-term rental articles:
  - One converts the zoning provisions to a general bylaw, which needs only a 50% vote and allows stronger enforcement.
  - The general bylaw covers more ineligible units, advertising and listing requirements, parking and noise rules, and a rental certificate that can be revoked after three material violations on separate events in a year.
  - A zoning extension of the sunset clause keeps the use legal, with a backup article if the others fail.
  - The proposed limit of one short-term rental per owner was removed after the public hearing and will be studied further.
  - Planning will recommend a January 1 effective date for the general bylaw and extending the zoning sunset to December 31, 2027.
- ADU article: updates the bylaw to the state mandate. Since January 2025 there have been 25 applications, 6 completed ADUs, 15 in review, and 4 withdrawn.
- The planning board returns on **October 27**, and the board asked that it be placed early on the agenda. Board members voiced frustration with state-level housing proposals. They said Senator Sear has agreed to meet with the board.

### Other business
- Open committee vacancies: board of appeals alternates, one conservation commission seat, and the community and economic development committee.
- **Executive session minutes: board acted on them (some held, some released).**
- **Consent agenda approved, with the foundation donation corrected to $31,000.**
- The meeting was **adjourned**.
- The town administrator said:
  - The FY28 budget process has started, with departmental budgets due November 2.
  - Revenues are stable but growth is limited, so there is little room for new services without voter approval (override or debt exclusion).
  - DEP approved the Bayberry Hills hydrogeological evaluation showing the site can handle the plant's 1.8 million gallon design flow, though some added costs are not in phase one.
  - A wastewater update with the advisory committee is planned by mid-November, and a November 10 capital workshop is planned.
  - New IT and economic development directors have started.

## Takeaways

- **Public safety costs are likely to rise:** The fire study points to more staff, new or replaced stations and urgent safety fixes. Combined with the budget message, residents should expect debates over priorities and possible ballot questions rather than quick tax-funded growth.
- **Short-term rentals and ADUs are headed for town meeting:** The proposed bylaws add stricter rules and enforcement, but a limit on owning multiple rentals is not included. Residents can weigh in on October 27, and the state's ADU and duplex rules are narrowing what the town can regulate.
- **Wastewater and services keep moving:** The town got good news on the Bayberry Hills discharge site, and the town is pre-funding sewer work with a proposed $2 million transfer. A regional 911 center, funded mainly by state grants, aims to improve dispatch times and could lower dispatch costs over time.
