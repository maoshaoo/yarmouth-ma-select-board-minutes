## Summary

The Select Board's busiest agenda items were two presentations and a review of the fall town meeting warrant. After a closed session on strategy for collective bargaining with the police supervisors union (roll call vote, all ayes), the board heard from the executive director of the planned Cape Cod Regional Emergency Communication Center. It then heard a long presentation from the Center for Public Safety Management (CPSM) on its fire department "standards of cover" study. The study found Yarmouth's fire and EMS service professional and well rated, but short of national response benchmarks. It recommended a gradual staffing increase over ten years, a possible fourth fire station, and urgent fixes at the aging stations. Board members called the report valuable but said the cost was a shock, and they asked for priorities and cost estimates once the fire chief's separate feasibility study is done.

The board also did a second review of the draft warrant for the upcoming special town meeting, which has eight articles: ambulance replacement, golf capital items, revolving funds, a wastewater stabilization deposit, three short-term rental articles and one accessory dwelling unit (ADU) article. The Planning Board's town planner explained the short-term rental and ADU articles. The board voted unanimously to adopt the Old King's Highway election warrant and set its nomination deadlines. It also approved a consent agenda with a donation figure corrected to $31,000.

In public comment, the Dennis-Yarmouth schools superintendent read a letter asking the board to rethink its budget policy. He said the schools have needed five overrides in ten years while the municipal budget has grown faster than the school assessment. The town administrator later said FY28 budget requests are due November 2 and that there is little room for new growth under Proposition 2½. He also reported that DEP approved the hydrogeological study for the Bayberry Hills groundwater discharge site.

## Key Points & Votes

### Executive session
- **Voted by roll call to enter executive session** to discuss collective bargaining strategy with the police supervisors union. All members voted aye.

### Public comment
- Superintendent Mark Smith presented a ten-year comparison. Over that period the district sought **five overrides**, its average yearly assessment increase was **3.96%**, and the municipal budget (Article 2) averaged **4.64%**. The municipal budget grew **$18.67 million** versus **$14.4 million** for the district assessment.
- Smith said the district cut **46 positions in three years** and asked the board to develop a long-term school funding strategy or, if it prefers overrides, to stand with the schools.
- A resident asked the board to look at a sidewalk and streetlight gap on Winslow Gray Road.
- A South Yarmouth resident criticized the state senator's duplex bill as unlikely to produce affordable housing.
- A school committee member (speaking for himself) urged the board to review recent school committee meetings and endorsed the communication center.

### Regional Emergency Communication Center
- Executive Director Joseph Creen said the center is a partnership of Barnstable (host), Yarmouth and Sandwich, and will be built on Finney's Lane in Barnstable. It will have 11 console positions, expandable to 15.
- Construction is estimated at **$15–20 million**, to be covered by a State 911 Department grant. The state judged the project not construction-ready last cycle, and the application is planned this year. He is eyeing opening in **2028–2029**, realistically 2030.
- State grants would cover the town's assessment at **100% for three years**, then **50%** in year four and **75%** in year five. The full share begins in year six.
- The assessment is based 50% on call volume and 50% on population. It may be around **$100,000**, but the figure is not yet firm. Creen expects a number within two to three months.
- Staff would be Barnstable employees, with working groups that include Yarmouth dispatchers. Creen said he wants to keep existing dispatchers. Board members asked about language translation, training on local streets and fire district participation.
- A board member said the town currently pays for two independent dispatch functions and that those costs would be redirected to the assessment. The board asked that the assessment be included in capital planning.

### Fire Department standards of cover (CPSM)
- Findings: ISO rating of **3**. Calls rose **5.5%** over three years (EMS about 5%, fire over 10%), and about **25%** of calls come from five or six facilities. About **57%** of calls overlap another call, and **6.5%** overlap three or more.
- Response times (90th percentile) versus national benchmarks:

| Measure | Yarmouth | Benchmark |
|---|---|---|
| Call processing | 132 seconds | 64 seconds |
| EMS turnout | 96 seconds | 60 seconds |
| Fire turnout | 162 seconds | 80 seconds |
| Fire travel | 474 seconds | 240 seconds |

- Without mutual aid, Yarmouth cannot assemble the benchmark number of firefighters for residential, strip mall or apartment fires. About **32.9%** of the town is within 240 seconds of a station.
- Recommendations:
  - Adopt 240/360-second travel benchmarks and a 10-minute (600-second) full response benchmark.
  - Begin planning a fourth fire station.
  - Add a dedicated EMS unit and raise tower staffing from three to four.
  - Phase staffing from **13 to 18, and eventually 21 per shift** over ten years.
  - Equip engines for advanced life support.
  - Consider inspections of short-term rentals, which would add about 2,000 inspections a year.
  - Look at community paramedicine for frequent 911 users.
- Stations 1 (1960) and 2 (1940) were flagged for life-safety issues, including no second means of egress from sleeping quarters. Station 1 is in a hurricane slosh zone.
- Board discussion:
  - Members called the report valuable but a shock, and questioned the cost, a fourth station and how other Cape towns compare.
  - They discussed fees or bylaws for facilities that generate repeated calls, and a possible rate schedule for some repeated calls.
  - **The board asked the town administrator to look into the cost of an outdoor stairway or a rental as a stopgap for the second egress.**
  - The fire chief said a separate feasibility study of station options is under way, and the board expects short- and long-term priorities afterward.

### Old King's Highway election
- **Approved unanimously the warrant for the November 30, 2026 annual meeting and election.**
- **Approved unanimously nomination deadlines:** papers available through Friday, November 20 at 4:30 p.m.; returned by Tuesday, November 24 at 4:30 p.m.

### Town meeting warrant (second draft review, no votes)
- Eight articles. Article 1 is ambulance replacement paid from ambulance receipts. The ambulance has an order lead time of roughly 800 days. Golf capital items (Bass River and Bayberry bathrooms, cart paths) are also on the fall warrant so work can be done in the off-season.
- The revolving fund bylaw would set up funds for the fire training facility and Parkers River Landing park.
- Another article would transfer **$2 million** of free cash, not yet certified, to the wastewater stabilization fund.
- Short-term rentals (three articles):
  - Move the rules from zoning to a general bylaw, which needs a simple majority.
  - Add enforcement by health and police.
  - Allow loss of a rental certificate after three material violations in a calendar year.
  - Require listing and advertising consistent with town rules.
  - Keep the zoning sunset extension articles as backups.
  - The proposed one-rental-per-owner limit was dropped for now.
  - Planners will propose a January 1 effective date for the general bylaw and extending the zoning sunset to December 31, 2027.
- ADUs: the article updates the bylaw to comply with state law. Since January 2025 there have been 25 applications, 6 completed ADUs, 15 active and 4 withdrawn.
- Board members expressed concern about commercialization of residential neighborhoods and about state preemption. The Planning Board returns October 27.

### Other business
- **Approved the consent agenda** with a Last Call Foundation donation corrected to **$31,000**.
- Approved motions on executive session minutes (held or released).
- Open vacancies: Board of Appeals alternates, one Conservation Commission seat, and the Community and Economic Development Committee.
- Adjourned.

### Town administrator update
- FY28 budget materials went out to departments. Requests are due **November 2**. He said revenues are stable but growth is limited, and new initiatives would need voter approval (debt exclusions or overrides).
- DEP approved the hydrogeological evaluation showing Bayberry Hills can handle the **1.8 million gallons** of effluent the plant is designed for. Additional costs not in phase one remain, and a wastewater update is planned for mid-November.
- New IT and economic development directors have started.

## Takeaways

- **Public safety costs are likely to rise.** The fire study points to more firefighters per shift, possible new or replaced stations and urgent code fixes. These would compete for money under Proposition 2½ limits, so expect capital-plan and budget workshops (including a November 10 session) before any decisions.
- **Regional dispatch is expected to be grant-funded at first.** The state is expected to pay for construction and the town's early assessments, with the town's full share starting around year six. The board wants the eventual assessment budgeted ahead of time, and opening is still years away.
- **Housing and rental rules are tightening, with more to come.** The fall town meeting will take up short-term rental enforcement and state-driven ADU changes. The board and planners signaled further steps on limiting short-term rentals, and the board is pressing state legislators about housing and zoning bills. Wastewater work is also moving, with DEP approving the Bayberry Hills discharge study.
