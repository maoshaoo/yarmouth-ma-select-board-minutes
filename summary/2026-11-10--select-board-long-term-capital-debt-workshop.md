## Summary

The Select Board held a joint workshop with the Finance Committee and Capital Budget Committee on long-term capital planning and debt management. Town staff presented five major building projects in the 10-year capital plan: the Bayberry Hills groundwater recharge project (about **$25 million**), a new M.E. Small elementary school (about **$46 million**), a library placeholder (about **$41.6 million**), a fire station project (about **$40 million**), and wastewater Phase 2 (about **$29 million** net). The town's current debt service is about **$1 million a year**. Under the hypothetical schedule it would rise to nearly **$8 million a year** by 2037, when all five projects would be on the tax rate. The fire station is the largest driver because no state subsidy program exists for it. Staff stressed that all figures are rough planning estimates, and that the projects would be funded through debt exclusions voted by residents.

Staff and committee members discussed ways to smooth the spike, such as setting a "level" annual debt service target (examples shown at $7 million, with members suggesting $4–6 million). Other ideas were a capital stabilization override to pre-fund projects, one-year capital exclusions, deferring projects, and a residential tax exemption. At a 15% exemption, the average home (assessed at $712,400) would save about **$315** a year. A non-resident owner of the same home would pay about **$492** more, and the break-even point is roughly **$1.2 million** in value. Opinions were mixed. Several board members favored studying or adopting the exemption, while some committee members worried about the effect on second homeowners and short-term rental revenue. Others raised the impact on year-round renters and suggested a way to include landlords who rent to locals. The finance director said free cash is declining and is already going to the wastewater stabilization fund, with **$2 million** on the town meeting warrant this year and last.

On priorities, several members objected to keeping the library in the plan after voters rejected the project, though one member strongly defended it. Members also wanted the plan to cover wastewater beyond Phase 1, and one said the town needs a way to explain how the full program will be paid for. Others asked for a firmer short-term cost for making the existing fire stations safe, and there was broad support for a level debt service approach, concern for the town's bond rating, and support for getting children out of M.E. Small. One board member said he opposes the Bayberry Hills discharge in the aquifer protection district except as a last resort. The board asked staff to return with a plan and next steps, and members suggested reconvening before spring.

Earlier, the board unanimously endorsed the appointment of Lujiri Cook as Treasurer/Collector, following the recent retirement of the prior treasurer. A Cape Light Compact representative also gave an update on energy efficiency programs and rebates.

## Key Points & Votes

### Treasurer/Collector appointment
- **The board voted unanimously to endorse the appointment of Lujiri Cook as Treasurer/Collector.**
- Cook has served about six or seven years as assistant town accountant. The finance team recruited and chose her unanimously.

### Cape Light Compact update
- Compact staff said the compact's supply rate is often below Eversource's basic service rate, though not always. Residents should not share account numbers with third-party suppliers.
- Residents can get home energy assessments and income-based enhanced incentives.
- Current offers include **100% off insulation** for customers heating with oil, propane, or electricity.
- Small businesses can get up to **100%** coverage for HVAC, insulation, refrigeration, lighting, and controls, and up to **50%** for heat pumps. Nonprofits can get more.
- The 0% heat loan (up to **$25,000**) will change next year.

### Major capital projects (planning estimates, no votes)
- **Bayberry Hills groundwater recharge:** about **$25 million**, bringing the plant to 1.8 million gallons a day of recharge. Net cost to the town is about **$12 million** after state subsidies, 0% SRF financing, and an assumed 20% betterment. Staff said it was moved up because of DEP's reluctance to permit more recharge at the existing site.
- **M.E. Small elementary school:** about **$46 million**, net about **$19 million** with an estimated 57% state building authority share. A feasibility study is under way and includes a no-build option. Tax impact is about 12 cents per $1,000.
- **Library:** placeholder moved out three years and escalated to **$41.6 million**, net about **$22.88 million**, about 14 cents per $1,000, assuming a 45% state grant that members called uncertain.
- **Fire station project:** about **$40 million**, based on comparable towns, with no subsidy. Station 1 (1965) is overcrowded, and Station 3 on Buck Island Road was never finished. A study also suggested a possible fourth station, which staff said is premature.
- **Wastewater Phase 2:** based on the old plan (about **$60 million**, 1,950 customers), net about **$29 million**, about 8 cents per $1,000. The configuration is being re-analyzed, with preliminary results expected in six to eight months. Staff said some property tax backstop will likely be needed.

### Debt management strategies discussed
- A level debt service target, a capital stabilization override, one-year capital exclusions, project deferral, and free cash set-asides.
- Residential exemption (10% to 35% allowed). Staff noted it needs ongoing administration, possibly one full-time employee.
- A split tax rate was discussed but staff said commercial value is only about 5% of the base, so it would have little effect.
- Illustrative average-bill increases if nothing is done: about **$24** (2029), **$178** (2033), **$329** (2035), and **$429** (2037).
- Yarmouth has the second-lowest average tax bill on Cape Cod. Its borrowing is far below the statutory limit.

### Other business
- **Minutes of February 3, 2026 were approved.**
- A special town meeting is next week, with a short executive session on pending litigation on November 16 before the 5 p.m. meeting.
- Upcoming: a fee discussion December 8 and the new capital improvement program December 15.
- The town administrator is arranging a meeting with DEP's deputy commissioner on Buck Island Road groundwater discharge and will notify the lieutenant governor's and governor's offices.
- A board member apologized to colleagues over remarks made at a Cape Cod Commission hearing.
- **The meeting was adjourned.**

## Takeaways

- **Taxes:** Debt service is projected to rise from about $1 million to nearly $8 million a year by 2037, which could add several hundred dollars a year to the average tax bill. Officials are exploring ways to level this out, including pre-funding, a level debt target, and a residential exemption.
- **Wastewater and development:** Phase 1 financing is considered solid, but Phase 2 and later phases lack a full funding plan. Several members want those phases included in long-term planning, and the town is pressing DEP on groundwater discharge limits.
- **Town services and facilities:** The fire stations and M.E. Small school have real needs, while the library's place in the plan is contested. Staff will return with more cost detail, a reconvened workshop, and an operating budget and revenue forecast review.
